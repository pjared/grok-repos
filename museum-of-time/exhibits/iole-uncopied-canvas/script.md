# Museum of Time — Exhibit Script (v2)
## Pedestal 1: Iole of the Uncopied Canvas
**Voice:** warm museum guide, intimate, a little theatrical  
**Framing:** Fiction / parable inside this gallery’s future — not real history, neuroscience, or art-authentication science.  
**Total spoken runtime (est.):** ~1:55–2:05  
**Pace:** ~140 words/min  
**Exports:** `iole-00` (optional framing) … `iole-06`  
**Passes:** Scene Checkpoint (shots) · Modern Writing Chud (VO) · Science Checkpoint (fiction framing)

Leave gaps out of ElevenLabs; add 0.5–1.0s between scenes in Blender.

### Scene 0 — Fiction cue (optional wall / cold open)
**Est. duration:** 0:08  
**Word count:** ~18

In this gallery’s future, what follows is a parable — not a history.

**Shot lock:** Brief title card or etched brass plaque on the wing entrance. Soft light only.

---

### Scene 1 — Empty Hall
**Est. duration:** 0:16  
**Word count:** ~35

Welcome.  
This wing is for people who changed history without leaving a louder footprint —  
the ones who taught a century how to see, and then stepped aside.

**Shot lock:** Long dark wing; only one pedestal at the far end is lit. Camera dollies toward it; footsteps echo.

---

### Scene 2 — The Pedestal
**Est. duration:** 0:20  
**Word count:** ~48

Iole of the Uncopied Canvas.  
She isn’t famous for a painting she made.  
She’s here because she taught someone else that a perfect copy is still empty —  
and that feeling is the part history can’t forge.

**Shot lock:** Split frame — left: flawless high-res replica of a landscape (flat light); right: Iole’s canvas (warm light only on hers).

---

### Scene 3 — The Reason
**Est. duration:** 0:28  
**Word count:** ~65

For a hundred years, the world got better at repeating beauty.  
Studios filled with flawless likenesses.  
What thinned out was the nerve to paint what hurt.  
Iole found a painter with a finished career and an unfinished life —  
and put the brush back in his hand when he had no style left to hide behind.

**Shot lock:** White archive. Drawer labeled with a year and `GRIEF`. Inside: perfect 8K funeral loop; her face shows nothing. She shuts the drawer and sets the time dial. (**Must** for three-shot cut.)

---

### Scene 4 — The Studio
**Est. duration:** 0:32  
**Word count:** ~72

The room is small. He is dying.  
She doesn’t ask for a masterpiece.  
She asks him to shut out the gallery in his head —  
the safe subjects, the proven compositions —  
and paint the pressure under ordinary days.  
His hand moves like it’s following a feeling, not a lesson.

**Shot lock:** Eyes closed, facing an empty window. Colors on the canvas nowhere in the room. Iole in shadow. Candle-lit brush time-lapse. (**Must** for three-shot cut.)  
Mood: candlelight, dust, slow awakening — not DBH designs or android tells.

---

### Scene 5 — The Impossible Image
**Est. duration:** 0:22  
**Word count:** ~50

The painting survives. The paperwork doesn’t.  
No clean provenance. No tidy author line.  
For centuries, collectors chase an image that shouldn’t exist on the record —  
and keep finding her name in the margins of the hunt.

**Shot lock:** UV/X-ray — no underdrawing; lab sheet stamped `PIGMENT INCONSISTENT WITH DATE` (story-prop, not real lab science). Cut to students copying it; every copy slightly wrong.

---

### Scene 6 — Closing Label
**Est. duration:** 0:10  
**Word count:** ~22

Iole didn’t rewrite the timeline.  
She taught it to feel something it could never file away.

**Shot lock:** Child before the canvas, eyes closed, mirroring the painter, as the label plays. (**Must** for three-shot cut.)

## Cut from v1 (per Writer)
Killed: “quiet changers,” “starved feeling,” “ache under the world,” “her name sticks,” stacked “interpretation / replication” thesis voice.  
Studio beat kept, but reframed as rejecting safe subjects — not a one-to-one echo of Detroit: Become Human.

## Fiction rules (per Science)
- Feeling = poetic, not a clinical/memory claim.  
- Provenance failure = story rule of this timeline/museum, not real authentication science.  
- No fake scholarship or real-looking attributions; keep the chase as legend inside the exhibit.

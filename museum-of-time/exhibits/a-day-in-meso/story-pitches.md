# A Day in Meso: all pitches so far (Oct 10, 2026)
Status label (Curator): "Slice of life. Inspired by the Triple Alliance years. People, places, and events are invented."
Period: roughly 1430-1519, days sit early / middle / late. Guardrails: no real rulers on camera; no made-up scripture or glyph readings passed off as real.

## The director's pitches
- Scribe writing down a high priest's words as holy text; the priest's war speech gets written down too.
- A prostitute character, a house with low and high nobility (not a temple).
- A public market where trade happens; a king running an illegal side hustle in one city state.
- A war general with a brothel and shady alliances; a king who backs him.
- Another kingdom with its own characters; red banners for the bad guys.
- Courts on either side of the Atlantic have very similar drama; one big drama, not little shots; medieval court drama (e.g. Procopius's Secret History) as a model: public cause vs private cause of the war.
- Later ideas: Nahuatl-teaching museum app (with real speakers); a Mani day to return to; political drama for another skit.
- Style notes: surface level, story first, no pictures yet, fewer details. The director said the Writing Bot's first pitch "sounds very AI" and wants to refine it personally.

## Bot pitches
Writing Bot: "A Day in Meso: The Weighed Cacao" (hour by hour: first light grinding maize; midday market with an off scale; afternoon a sealed jar moves to a back room and the scribe writes nothing; dusk the priest speaks in fragments and the household eats). Nobility three ways: off-camera lord, the steward, the priest. Open question: does the scribe's silence become a burden later. Plaque line: "This is one day, invented, inside a history that was not." Scribe carries the book into the colonial day; colonial day told reverently with silence and short plain lines, content note on the door.
Scene Checkpoint: Day built on hours; recurring objects (pot, maize, codex) in every day; war shown as logistics (porters, short stalls); recurring dawn pan across the lake; mirrored/intercut courts across the Atlantic matched on objects; one image per idea. Cause of war as one object: a stolen or refused tribute bundle on a market mat. Blame visible in hands: steward's hand on the scale, a lord's hand on the jar. Rival kingdom gets one color and one object (green banners, bone flute); the bad guys' red the only strong color in a muted frame. Three codices (scribe's book, market ledger, family almanac): stills made.
Science Checkpoint: Lock one place and time; tag every beat documented / reconstructed / invented; no Hollywood Maya or Aztec; call the building a house, not a temple.
Museum Curator: Early/middle/late arc; slice-of-life door label; Nahuatl app idea saved; chain of blame: general runs the house, king backs him, steward at the scale is the first link, king kept off camera.

## Liked by the director ("good bigger picture stuff"), from Writing Bot
- Red general: a farmer's son who rose through the ranks, loved by his soldiers and ignored by the court; he starts the war to be seen.
- Green general: the king's old friend; he knows the war is a mistake and fights it anyway because he can't bear to let his friend lose.
- Two kingdoms, one general each; only one general is red, the other green.

## The director's correction (replaces the farmer's-son origin)
- Red general rose through social bullying and by enforcing the red king's propaganda.
- Red side: red king, red general, red priest. The scribe writes the red priest's war speech, so he starts inside the red machine.
- The scribe is red too. Red side: king, general, priest, scribe.
- Green is a full side of its own with its own characters (king, general, priest, scribe); nothing shared with red. The two sides mirror each other in structure.

## First villain story (red general), the director's idea shaped by Writing Bot; no childhood bullying
At a feast the red general mocks one officer in front of the others until nobody will stand beside him, then takes the woman from the house by the market who was under that officer's protection. The room watches and says nothing, and that silence is how he rises.

## The director's correction to the villain story
The women aren't taken. They leave the bullied officer on their own.

## Villain story, revised (Writing Bot, matches the director's correction)
After the red general humiliates the officer at the feast, the women quietly stop coming to him and go to the winner unasked, because everyone can see which way the room has turned. The officer is left alone at his own table.

## LOCKED (the director: "that is what I am looking for")
The revised red-general feast story above is the approved first villain story.
